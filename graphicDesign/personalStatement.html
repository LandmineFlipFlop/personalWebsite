Personal Statement

For this section of my portfolio, I decided to reuse a personal statement I wrote for my Eagle Scout application:

Hi my name is Ely, my first experience with scouting was in joining my church’s cub pack. In pack 579 I got to know other youth, had a lot of fun but I was never truly committed. I eventually earned my Arrow of Light and had to make a tough choice, the church had announced that they would be dropping scouting as an official program. So me and my dad made the decision to find a troop in Eugene to join. After looking at several options we eventually decided on a small little tight knit troop, unit 61. In the troop I got to know some awesome people and got to truly know what scouting had to offer. With the pandemic having struck, staying active in scouts was a struggle but I ended up pushing through and getting my Second and even First Class ranks in 2021. Following that now that I had some momentum I was able to push and get Star rank in 2022 and Life in 2023. All that was left to do was one rank, how hard could it be I naively thought.

The majority of my journey towards Eagle was spent on merit badges and wondering how the heck I was going to do a project. My biggest struggle was balancing scout work and school, but I eventually got to a point where I had one summer left before I turned eighteen, two merit badges to finish, and one project to do. I ended up reaching out to the elementary school I went to and arranged to make two picnic tables for the school garden after the old ones had gotten worn down and vandalized. The whole project was a journey and I learned a lot from it about hard work, commitment, and leadership. Through my whole scouting journey I’ve gone from a brand new scout who had no clue what they were doing, to someone who has experience and can get stuff done.

The merit badge system in scouting has taught me a lot about things I never would have experienced. For example I never would have tried geocaching, or learned to unswamp a canoe if it weren't for merit badges. They’ve taught me a lot of very useful skills, such as meal balancing, gun safety, game design, car repairs, first aid, programming, film-making and more. Merit badges are the reason I know about some skills that a lot of kids, even adults don't. It was truly a great learning experience.

As of right now my plan is to finish my senior year with decent grades, and to go to Lane Community College next year. My goal is to keep the momentum and experience I’ve gotten from scouting and use what I’ve learned from scouts to become the best that I can. While scouting at the most surface level is simply a place to hang out with like minded youth and to learn some survival skills, if you truly engage in what it has to offer I’ve found that it teaches lessons and skills that I will use for the rest of my life.
